# Data Collection

## Castaway CTD 

We used the Castaway CTD to collect values of conductivity, temperature, and depth (see figure). 
The Castaway CTD (Conductivity, Temperature, and Depth) is an oceanographic instrument designed to collect critical water column data, including salinity, temperature, and depth measurements. Compact and user-friendly, the Castaway CTD is typically used for profiling in both shallow and deep waters. The device works by measuring conductivity to determine salinity, temperature through a thermistor, and depth via a pressure sensor. These sensors are housed in a small, portable unit that can be easily deployed from boats, piers, or other platforms. As the instrument is lowered through the water column, it continuously records these measurements, providing a vertical profile of the water's physical properties.

![Alt Text](../figures/datacollection/CTD_Diagram.jpeg)  
*Figure 1: Diagram of the Castaway CTD showing its components and how it functions.*

The Castaway CTD operates by leveraging its onboard sensors that interact with the water environment. The conductivity sensor measures the electrical conductivity of the water, which is then used to calculate salinity. The temperature is measured using a high-precision thermistor, while depth is determined through a piezoelectric pressure transducer. The data is collected at a user-defined sampling rate, which can range from 1 to 5 seconds, and the device’s memory can store several thousand data points. Data is later downloaded via USB or Bluetooth to a computer or mobile device, where it can be analyzed using proprietary software. The Castaway CTD is typically calibrated for accuracy and precision before field deployment, and settings such as sampling rate, data logging intervals, and time stamps can be adjusted to suit the specific requirements of the survey. This makes it a versatile and reliable tool for oceanographers, hydrologists, and environmental scientists studying water properties across various marine environments.

## Survey Parameters 

We sampled by 0.15 decibars of pressure which corresponds to a depth of around 1.53 mm or 0.00153 meters in freshwater using the formula:
 
$$ P = \rho g h $$

Where: 
- $P$ is the pressure in pascals (Pa) (1 dbar = 100 pascals)
- $\rho$ is the density of freshwater (~ 1000 $\frac{kg}{m^3}$)
- $g$ is the acceleration due to gravity (9.81 $\frac{m}{s^2}$)
- $h$ is the depth of the water column in meters

After setting up our sample rate on the CTD, we can then start either a cast or point measurement.

## Cast Measurement 

A cast measurement uses our sampling rate from survey parameters (link) to measure Pressure (Decibar), Depth (Meters), Temperature (Celsius), Conductivity (microSiemens per centimeter). The cast is started by someone on the boat using the CTD's interface, and the CTD is placed into the water and begins to sink. Our procedure consisted of one person sliding the rope and feeling for when the CTD hits the bottom, one person holding onto the spool and coiling the line up to ensure the line doesn't get tangled when the CTD is being reeled back in, and another person taking notes on the data collected in real-time. 

![Group recording data off the CTD after a cast measurement](../figures/datacollection/grp4note.jpg)  
*Figure 3: Group recording data after a cast measurement.*

The note-taker recorded the time, depth, and station number being recorded on a GPS app on their phone (Garmin GPS Site) as well as any changes in the procedure, data quality, and weather.

After a cast is taken, the results are confirmed to be valid by looking at the profiles on the interface and seeing values of temperature and conductivity varying with depth, and depth that is deemed appropriate based on the assumed bathymetric patterns of a reservoir. If the data looks valid, the group then moves to another area selected based on group consensus and the crew chief's decision on what data could be scientifically interesting.

## Point Measurement 

Point Measurements are taken by continuously collecting data at a singular depth by manually moving the CTD either laterally or vertically for a sample duration between 5-10 seconds. The CTD takes multiple measurements of conductivity, temperature, and pressure at a rate of 5 Hz (five times per second).

## Areas of Interest

The motivation behind our study at Stagecoach Reservoir is to collect detailed water column profiles from various locations around the reservoir. Specifically, we focus on comparing inlet-fed coves, such as Harding, with non-inlet coves, like Keystone, to assess how the lake behaves spatially. Additionally, casts are taken at the center of the reservoir during return trips. The data was collected at docks daily to understand Stagecoach's temporal variability.
